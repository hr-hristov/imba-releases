Ultrafocus

A higher level of focus, with just enough information.

imba runs many coding agents at once, one spawn each. A spawn that stops to wait for you does no work until you answer it. With thirty at work your mind slips in known ways, and each part of Ultrafocus guards against one of them.

On a phone, each key on this page is a tap.

01Waiting

The trap

“I'll answer the one that just pinged.”

Salience bias. The loudest thing on screen gets your attention, not the one that matters.

Only what waits on you.

Ctrl+U, or the crosshair on the bar, clears away the 22 spawns at work, and Automations, Alarms and Tasks with them. The 7 waiting on you stay, each with the number that opens it.

02One line

The trap

“Seven replies. I'll skim them.”

Information overload. Faced with too much, you skim, and a skim ends in a yes.

Just enough on a row.

On a laptop each row carries one sentence: what its last turn set out to do, ticked if it got there and crossed if not. A phone keeps the name alone.

7 waiting22 at work

03Proof

The trap

“It said it's done.”

Automation bias. A confident machine's word gets trusted over your own eyes.

The proof, not the essay.

With a reply set to Pictures, films and cards in Settings, 1 opens the refund email on its two pictures. t, or Full reply on a phone, brings its three paragraphs back, and w sends it back to work.

04Next

The trap

“Which one should I take next?”

Decision fatigue. Every choice of what comes next spends a little judgement, and by the tenth you choose badly.

Press 1, answer, back.

The rows stand in the order they started waiting, a repository at a time, so there is nothing to pick. 1 opens the top one, w sends it back to work, q comes back, and the next one is 1. The count at the foot climbs with each.

1 Openw Doneq Back

05Arrivals

The trap

“The newest one must be the most urgent.”

Recency bias. What happened last feels more pressing than what has waited longest.

New work joins the line.

When a spawn at work finishes its turn, its row joins its repository's group behind the ones that waited longer. A lap of light round the frame says so, and the count at work drops by one.

5 waiting24 at work

06Empty

The trap

“All of them are working. I'll start the next one later.”

Present bias. A small effort now outweighs a bigger payoff later, so the next start keeps slipping.

Nothing waiting. Start the next.

Later the last one goes back to work. Waiting closes and New takes the frame: each repository and the tasks queued for it, a number each. A number opens the start of the next spawn.

Every key and button

Each press, and its key.

The round above uses a few of them. Here is all of it: what you press on the screen, the key that does the same, and what happens. Keys need a keyboard; on a phone or tablet the buttons are taps.

Rows and headings

  • A row1–9Opens it: a spawn's reply, or the start of new work in a repository. Beside another view, New's numbers take Ctrl.
  • Working heading, or the rankiShows or hides the spawns at work.
  • Parked headingyShows or hides the spawns set aside.
  • Tasks headingcShows or hides the coding tasks queued under each repository in New.
  • Page 1/2]The next page, when the rows run past one.

At the foot of the frame

  • ViewswThe views list: Waiting, Working, Parked, New, Shells, Routines, Tasks. A press shows or hides a view; on a phone or tablet the view takes the screen.
  • The n square, or the New headingnKeeps New, the repositories to start work in, beside what waits, or takes it off. On a phone or tablet New takes the screen, and a second press brings Waiting back.
  • MachineoThe Machine list: Clone repository, Monitor, Docker, Agent usage, Files.
  • Clone repositoryrClones a repository onto the server. No spawn starts.
  • MonitormOpens the machine monitor.
  • DockerdThe Docker manager: containers, Compose stacks, images.
  • Agent usageuHow much of each agent plan's allowance is spent.
  • Fileso then fExplores the whole machine's files, from the Machine list.
  • The bellbThe sound when a spawn waits, on or off.
  • Settings, on a phone or tabletOpens Settings. A keyboard reaches it by the section key Settings wears on the app's bars.
  • Leave Ultrafocus, on a phone or tabletCtrl+ULeaves after a 15 second count on the foot line.

In an open list

  • j kThe next line, or the one before.
  • A lineEnterPicks it.
  • q EscCloses the list.

Letters that wait for a number

The letter's word stands on the foot line, and the next number acts on that row instead of opening it. The same letter again, or Esc, lets it go.

  • DeletexAsks before deleting the queued task.
  • FilesfThe repository's files. Explore files on a repository in New does the same.
  • GraphgThe repository's commit graph. Commit graph on a repository in New does the same.
  • MaphThe repository's structure, dependencies and churn. Repo map on a repository in New does the same.
  • PRspThe repository's pull requests.
  • PinkPins the repository to New, or unpins it.
  • LooplA new loop in the row's repository.
  • OrchestrationShift+LA new orchestration in the row's repository.
  • New taskaA coding task queued in the row's repository. Nothing starts.
  • ShellsA new shell in the row's repository.
  • Web linkvSaves a web link in the row's repository.
  • Add.The row's add menu.
  • RestorezPast work to restore in the row's repository.
  • Task listtThe spawn's task list.
  • EditeEdits the queued task.

Anywhere on the screen

  • The crosshair on the barCtrl+UUltrafocus on or off.
  • /A search box at the top.
  • Shift+/Every key, and where to change it.
  • Ctrl+Shift+NA new spawn with no repository.
  • Ctrl+SA new shell.

On an open reply

  • Full reply, or Proof onlytThe whole reply, or the proof alone.
  • wDone. The spawn goes back to work.
  • qBack to Ultrafocus.
